# Design Decisions

OBP-Cohort manages startup programmes on the Open Bank Project: calls for applications, applications, evaluation, onboarding, programme delivery, milestones and graduation. The monorepo's [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](../../DESIGN_DECISIONS.md) applies too.

## Architecture

- **OBP-API is the only backend.** Cohort has no database of its own. Its records are dynamic entities; users, login, roles, Customers, files, chat and email are OBP features.
- **One space per installation**, set by `OBP_ENTITY_SPACE_ID` (a bank id, default `cohort`, or `SYS`). Everything Cohort stores lives in it, except the answers to published forms.
- **A second space for forms**, set by `COHORT_FORMS_SPACE_ID` (default: the space id with `-forms`, e.g. `cohort-forms`). Each published form's dynamic entity and its answers live there. Roles are checked at a bank id, so the definition Roles that publishing needs (`CanCreateDynamicEntityDefinition`, `CanUpdateDynamicEntityDefinition`) are granted at the forms space and reach nothing of Cohort's own: applications, decisions, participants and Customers stay out of reach. Inside the forms space they still let their holder change any form's definition, so forms need trusted admins. References are ids, as everywhere in Cohort: answers point into Cohort's space (`subject_id`: an application or participant, named with its space in the definition), and Cohort's space points into the forms space only from the `cohort_form` record (`entity_name`). Nothing else crosses, so the forms space can be inspected, backed up or moved on its own. The bank must be created once, by an administrator; `/staff/setup` checks it.
- **Cohort is a Platform App.** It declares the Scopes it needs (`cohortRequiredConsumerScopes` in `packages/shared`) and, with its application token, creates its dynamic entities at startup and adds properties they lack. `/staff/setup` shows the space, the Scopes and the dynamic entities, and links to the API Manager pages that fix what is missing.
- **Dynamic entity definitions live in code** (`src/lib/server/entities`) and only grow: properties are added, never renamed, retyped or removed.
- **A third app in OBP-Frontend** (`apps/cohort`), built and deployed as its own image. Programme-specific code stays here; anything another app could use goes into `packages/shared`, never copied.

## Records

- **Programmes and calls are public records**, so the website lists open calls without login. Staff create them with the per-dynamic-entity Roles at the space.
- **Applications are the applicant's personal records** (`/my/`, no Role needed): a draft until submitted, so applicants save and resume. Staff read every application through the community URL (`CanGetDynamicEntityRecord_cohort_application` at the space), read only.
- **Staff decisions are separate, append-only records** (screening, evaluation, stage changes), with who, when and why. They never edit the applicant's record. The latest decision, of any stage, is the current outcome. Screening (shortlisted, rejected, needs_info) and evaluation (accepted, waitlisted, rejected) decisions are both `cohort_screening_decision` records, told apart by `stage`, so the applicant's outcome, the emails and the Roles stay one mechanism; the dynamic entity keeps its first name, as definitions are never renamed.
- **Applicants see the outcome, never the reasoning.** Cohort reads decisions with its own token (applicants hold no Role) and shows the applicant the decision and the message written for them, never the internal reason. "More information needed" reopens the application for editing and resubmission.
- **People and startups are Customers of the space**, linked to Users by User Customer Links. A User is the login; a Customer is the legal person. The first time someone applies, a "Your details" step (legal name, mobile, email) has Cohort, as itself, create an INDIVIDUAL Customer for them (kyc_status false) and the link; each application keeps its `customer_id`. An accepted startup becomes a CORPORATE Customer (address, tax residencies, KYC), and its team are linked to it.
- **Programme membership is a dynamic entity**, `cohort_participant`: a startup's place in a programme (`programme_id`, `application_id`, the applicant's `user_id`, the startup's `customer_id`, `stage`, `status`: active, withdrawn, graduated, and dates). Accepting an application at evaluation creates the participant at once, with the accepting staff member's Roles (Get and Create); Cohort reads and updates a startup's own participant with its Get and Update Scopes. On *My programme* the startup then registers its company, and Cohort creates the CORPORATE Customer, links the applicant to it and records its `customer_id`. Staff see every participant on `/staff/participants` with the Get Role. Milestones, sessions and attendance refer to it.
- **Milestones are records too.** A programme's milestones are public `cohort_milestone` records (title, what counts, due date). A startup reports on one with its own personal `cohort_milestone_report` (a note, evidence files attached to the report); staff accept it or return it with a message as an append-only `cohort_milestone_review`, with their own Roles. A milestone's status for a startup (not started, submitted, returned, accepted) follows from its newest report and their reviews; past its due date and not accepted, the startup is behind. Staff see the whole programme on `/staff/programmes/<id>/milestones`; a startup sees its own on My programme, the reviews read by Cohort and narrowed to its own participation.
- **Sessions are records too.** Staff plan a programme's sessions (`cohort_session`: mentoring, workshop or demo day, with time, length, place or link, mentor and seats) on `/staff/programmes/<id>/sessions`, and after a session starts they record who attended (`cohort_attendance`, append-only), all with their own Roles. A startup books a seat with its own personal `cohort_session_booking` (and cancels by deleting it) on My programme; Cohort reads its programme's sessions, the seats taken (a count, never who) and its own attendance with its Scopes, narrowed on the server. Sessions attended per startup feed the dashboard and its CSV.
- **Eligibility is checked on submission**: a local resident or a locally incorporated business is eligible; anyone else goes to a screener.
- **Stages are data, not code.** A programme has ordered stages; a gate is a set of conditions on a startup's records. There is no workflow engine. Today the stages are onboarded, in the programme and graduated: a startup starts the programme once its company is registered, and graduates once every milestone of its programme is accepted (`src/lib/stages.ts`). Staff move it on from the Participants page with their own Roles; Cohort checks the gate again on the server, updates the participant and adds an append-only `cohort_stage_change` (from, to, who, when, note).
- **The graduation certificate is a page, in the User's language.** A graduated startup opens its certificate from My programme and prints it or saves it as PDF. The browser lays out Arabic (joined letters, right to left) as well as English, which the plain PDF libraries cannot; a server-made PDF, when wanted, comes from a service that shapes Arabic, never from one that would break it. Arabic is a first-class language throughout.
- **Evaluation is records too.** A call's rubric is public `cohort_rubric_criterion` records (maximum score, weight). People offer to review with a personal `cohort_reviewer` record; staff accept or decline each offer with an append-only `cohort_reviewer_decision` (the latest is the standing, read by Cohort itself, as screening decisions are), and assign only accepted reviewers with `cohort_review_assignment` records, never edited. A reviewer's `cohort_review` is their own personal record: first a conflict-of-interest declaration (with a conflict, no scores, and staff reassign), then a score per criterion, comments and the weighted total out of 100. Staff rank a call's submitted applications by the average of their scored reviews, then record the outcome on the evaluation page: accepted, waitlisted or rejected.
- **Reviewers see only their assignments, once accepted.** Until staff accept an offer to review, the reviewer page shows only the offer and its standing. Then it shows only the caller's own assignments, and an application only after the reviewer has declared no conflict. The Roles reviewers hold (Get applications, Get assignments) are wider; the pages narrow them.
- **The management view is counted from the records, never stored.** `/staff/dashboard` counts each call's funnel (started, submitted, eligible, shortlisted, accepted, waitlisted, not selected, by each application's decisions) and each programme's startups (by stage, companies registered, milestones accepted out of all due, startups behind), read with the staff member's own Roles; each part needs its own Role and offers to request it. Both tables download as CSV (`funnel.csv`, `startups.csv`), read the same way.
- **One form builder and one renderer** for a call's extra application questions and for surveys of a programme's startups (`/staff/forms`). A form is a public `cohort_form` record holding its questions (kind, wording and guidance in English and Arabic, bilingual choices, required), edited freely while a draft. Publishing makes it its own dynamic entity (`cohort_form_<id>`) in the forms space, one typed property per question, so answers are typed, filterable and readable by Opey. Staff publish with their own Roles: `CanCreateDynamicEntityDefinition` at the forms space creates the dynamic entity and OBP-API grants them its Record Roles, so they read the answers at once; other staff request that dynamic entity's Get Role from the page. A published form only grows: a question added is added to the dynamic entity at once (`CanUpdateDynamicEntityDefinition`), a question can be hidden but never removed or retyped, and a bigger change is a new version (a new draft that, once published, closes the one it replaces). Each applicant or startup writes their own answers as a personal record of the form's dynamic entity (`subject_id`: the application or the participant), with no Role; staff read them all through its community URL, as a per-question summary and as CSV. An application records the form it answered (`form_id`); its answers appear after the standard fields for the applicant, staff and reviewers. Scoring rubrics stay `cohort_rubric_criterion` records for now.

## Files

- **Applicants upload their own files, with no staff in the way.** Attachments are part of the application form, and applying needs no staff contact, so the gate is automatic: a logged-in user with a validated email may upload personal files within a per-user quota (count and total size per space), of allowed types (PDF, Office documents, images), never public, each attached to one of their own records, such as a draft application.
- **Quarantined until scanned.** Each file has a `scan_status` (`pending`, `clean`, `rejected`) with when, by whom and why. Personal uploads start `pending`; uploads by holders of `CanCreateFileAtOneBank` start `clean` unless a prop says to scan everything. Until a file is `clean` its metadata is shown but its content is served only to its owner and to the scanner; a `rejected` file is never served.
- **One way to change the status:** `PUT /banks/BANK_ID/files/FILE_ID/scan-status`, guarded by `CanUpdateFileScanStatusAtOneBank`, which also lets its holder read the content of pending files. A scanner app holds it as a Scope and sets the result; staff use it to approve a file when no scanner is configured, to reject a reported file, and to clear a false positive.
- **Scanning is pluggable.** With a prop such as `files.clamav.host` set, OBP-API scans at upload with ClamAV and sets the status itself. Without it, files wait for a scanner app (the installation's own antivirus) or a member of staff.
- **Staff never see a file by surprise.** No inline previews or thumbnails of applicant files: staff see the name, type, size and scan status, and a file opens only on an explicit click, as a download. A "Report" action hides the file and stops the uploader uploading more.
- **Staff read applicant files with `CanGetFileAtOneBank`** at the space; access never follows from reading the record a file is attached to.

## Access control

- **RBAC only.** Per-dynamic-entity and per-field Roles at the space, granted through Role Groups. Jurisdiction is a field used in queries; subscription content is a Role Group granted on approval.
- **Staff act with their own Roles; Cohort's Scopes are only for what no staff member does.** Anything a member of staff does on purpose (deciding, accepting and onboarding, assigning reviewers, reading lists of other people's records) runs with their own token and Roles at the space, and the page offers to request a missing Role. Cohort's Consumer Scopes cover only its own setup and what Users do for themselves: registering as a Customer, registering their company, and seeing their own outcome, standing or participation. Everything read with Cohort's token is narrowed on the server to the logged-in User's own records, without staff-only fields such as a decision's reason, and the token never leaves the server. Those reads are the one place OBP-API's Entitlements do not protect, so each has a test (`cohortTokenReads.test.ts`).
- **OBP-API Entitlements are what protect data; the menu only follows them.** Logging in is needed for everything but the public pages. The Staff menu, its pages and the site map's Staff section are shown only to Users holding a staff Role at the space; anyone else opening a staff page is sent to `/staff/access` to request one. That is for clarity, not protection: every staff page reads with the User's own Roles.

## Frontend

- **Cohort users do everything in Cohort.** Startups, mentors, reviewers and programme staff never need the Portal or the API Manager; the API Manager is the technical admin console.
- **One sidebar on every page**, in the Portal's style, on the start side so it mirrors in Arabic. Visitors who are not logged in see only the public pages (`(public)`: open calls, a call, login) and a Log in button. Cohort has its own sidebar rather than the shared `NavigationSidebar`, whose text is English only.
- **List, view, edit.** Staff lists and detail pages need only read access. Editing is a separate page, guarded by the dynamic entity's update Role at the space; a User without it sees a request for the Role there instead of the form. Create and edit share one set of form fields, and a save replaces the whole record.
- **Branding is configuration, not code.** The name, logos, favicon, theme (`data-theme`) and an extra stylesheet each come from an env var (`COHORT_NAME`, `COHORT_LOGO_URL` and so on), else the OBP default. Branding is Cohort's own setting, so it is read from one place only, not also from a webui_prop. A brand theme is a stylesheet defining a Skeleton theme's CSS variables; URLs must be http(s) or paths on the site.
- **Opey on the open calls page**, beside the calls, when `OPEY_BASE_URL` is set. Visitors chat without logging in; each message carries the calls on the page, so answers are about them. A logged-in User's Opey acts through a consent the User holds. A logged-in User's conversations are recorded, as that User, into the personal dynamic entity `cohort_opey_conversation` in Cohort's space (the Portal's definition under Cohort's name): the audit log of questions and answers. Each User reads their own at `/conversations` (My Opey conversations: the personal records, no Role); auditors read all of them at `/staff/conversations` with its Get Role at the space. Both show plain text; anonymous chats are not recorded.
- **Reminders are sent by Cohort on its own.** Every `COHORT_REMINDER_INTERVAL_MINUTES` (default 60, 0 off) Cohort plans the reminders due (`src/lib/reminders.ts`): a milestone due within a week and not yet reported or accepted, a milestone overdue, and a call closing within three days on a draft application. It emails each to the startup's company, else its applicant, and records it once in `cohort_reminder`, so none is sent twice; a failed email is tried again next time. No staff member sends them, so Cohort uses its Scopes, and what it reads is never shown; staff see the log on `/staff/reminders` with the Get Role.
- **Emails go to Customers, through OBP-API.** Cohort tells applicants their application was received and when there is a decision (the outcome and the message for them, never the reason) with Send Email to Customer, as itself (`CanSendEmailToCustomerAtOneBank` Scope at the space). Only the space's own Customers can be emailed, OBP-API reads the address from the Customer, and its message outbox sends it, so Cohort never handles SMTP. Each email is in English and Arabic, as plain text. A failed email is logged; what the User did still counts.
- **Example data** (`/staff/example-data`) adds example programmes, calls and applications with the logged-in User's Roles; the applications are that User's own. Adding again skips what exists.
- **SvelteKit, Svelte 5, TypeScript.** OBP calls are made server-side.
- **English and Arabic, including RTL.** All UI text goes through `t()` (`src/lib/i18n`). The locale comes from the `cohort_locale` cookie, then Accept-Language, and sets `lang` and `dir` on `<html>`. Layouts use logical CSS properties.
- **Accessible** (WCAG 2.1 AA): semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, contrast.

## Deployment

- **Runs on-premises**, with no external SaaS: OBP-API, OBP-OIDC, Redis, Postgres, Opey and OBP-Cohort.
- **Postgres**, because indexed dynamic entity queries need it.
